# The second recording (transcript C): review, October 2026

The second conversation between William Somerville (the narrator, b. 1937) and his grandson Gergely (the interviewer) was recorded on 28 October 2026, the day after the first. It was spent over the family's papers and, at a neighbouring family house, its portraits. This report compares every significant claim in it with:
- the two transcripts of the first recording;
- the archive as it stood;
- the October 2026 audit of the family's Ancestry tree;
- independent sources.

It also records what was changed in the archive. The claim-by-claim register is [`transcript-c-claims-2026-10.csv`](transcript-c-claims-2026-10.csv).

## 2. How the evidence was handled

- **Who said it.** Transcript C has no speaker labels. Each claim is attributed from context to one of four voices:
  - the **narrator** speaking from memory;
  - the **narrator reading a document aloud** (the Bible, the confirmation of arms, the cutting, the notebooks, the pedigree);
  - the **interviewer** (his own research, or reading from a screen);
  - the **narrator's written profiles**, read by the two of them together.

  The archive says which. For example, the Bâby family's Detroit origins come from the interviewer. The cause of the Youghal riot comes from the narrator's profile.
- **Labels.** Labels follow the archive's rules:
  - *Testimony* for what was said or read aloud.
  - *Published record* for what a source checked here states.
  - *Interpretation* for our inferences.
  - *Conflict* where documents disagree and neither is decisive.
  - *Family information* for the private tree.

  A document read aloud is evidence of what the narrator read, not a verified record. Where a reading can be compared with the 1897 pedigree, it has been.
- **One transcript, so numbers need care.** No second transcript exists to catch mishearings, so dates read aloud are treated with special caution. Three dates in the Bible, as heard, contradict the 1897 pedigree. They are kept as readings and an open question, not adopted.
- **Missing evidence.**
  - Where no record was found, the claim stays as testimony and an open question names the record that would settle it.
  - Nothing is invented to fill a gap; unknown dates are left blank.
  - A source that could not be opened is labelled "search result only" and never described as inspected.
  - Living people outside the narrator's immediate family are not profiled. What was said about them stays in the transcript.
  - Disputed claims keep both versions, with the reasoning.
- **Incorrect information.**
  - An entry was added only where stronger evidence contradicts something said.
  - An existing entry was revised where new evidence changed its basis.
  - A mere uncertainty is never added.

## 3. Key additions

1. **The family's 1897 pedigree** (*Visitation of Ireland*). This is the "Crisp's" of both recordings, and the narrator showed the family's annotated copy (C¶13–24). It gives the birth or baptism of four generations with places, wills and burials. Among other things:
   - It proves that Captain William's ten children were "every single one born at a different place", as the narrator said: Leixlip, Kilkenny, Dover, Carrick-on-Suir, Athboy, Lucan, Newry, Dundalk, Loughbrickland and Cumry.
   - It names the eighth son.
   - It shows that every child of Bellingham Arthur was baptised at St Audoen's, Dublin.
2. **The family Bible** (C¶25–34). Captain William entered his ten children "as they were born or baptized". An envelope holds a page of the original record, Tenison Alan's copy of 1850, and his record of his own children.
3. **The confirmation of arms** (C¶76–87). It was obtained by **Elizabeth Mary ("Lily") Somerville**, only child of **Henry Bellingham Somerville**, District Inspector, RIC. The National Library catalogue dates it **2 April 1924**. The arms and motto ("Crains Dieu tant que tu viveras", "Fear God in life") are those printed in 1897, which were not previously on record in the Ulster Office.
4. **The baronetcy succession** (C¶71–75, 131–144, 177–189).
   - The press in 1929 named **Bunt** heir to the baronetcy.
   - Reginald began a claim in the 1950s; the narrator is pursuing it with the College of Arms, where the officer named, **Phillip Bone, Rouge Dragon Pursuivant**, is confirmed.
   - His case is that Archibald James's 1836 marriage was bigamous, and that Corry's only son died childless.
   - The 1897 pedigree adds that **William Henry died without children in 1847**.
5. **Corry's branch** (C¶177–183). The narrator's own research found that Corry married Lucinda in Dublin before they went to Rochester, New York, and that their only son, William Bellingham Somerville, died childless.
6. **The senior line and Somerville House** (C¶51–64, 88–130).
   - The 2nd Baron Athlumney's career, as given in his obituary.
   - His Australian wife, **Margery Boan**.
   - His will, which brought the estate to **Quentin Agnew-Somerville**. This resolves the first recording's "Quentin Somerville debacle".
7. **The portraits** (C¶236–275):
   - **Colonel Thomas Bellingham**, guide to William III at the Boyne;
   - the two **Henry Bellinghams, MPs**;
   - **Sir Marcus Somerville**;
   - **Judge Tennison**, very probably Eliza Bellingham's great-grandfather;
   - **Daniel O'Connor of Shoreham**;
   - **John Williams Clinch**;
   - **Monique Bâby**;
   - miniatures of Sir James, Major William, Captain William and Eliza;
   - "probably **Wicked Alan**, son of Henry Bellingham".
8. **New people (17).**
   - Lily Somerville and her mother Mary Dyas.
   - Dorothea Riall (Mrs Richard Moore).
   - Corry's son William Bellingham.
   - Colonel Thomas Bellingham, Abigail Handcock, and the two Henry Bellinghams.
   - Judge Thomas Tennison and his son Richard Tenison.
   - John Williams Clinch.
   - Margery Boan, Harry Boan, Enid Boan and Sir Peter Agnew.
   - Toby Truell.
   - The Rev. Thomas Forster Rolfe.

### Corrections to the archive's own earlier conclusions

- **The RIC "elder brother"** (¶12, B¶6) was **Henry Bellingham Somerville**, not William Dennistoun as the archive had guessed. William Dennistoun ("Uncle Billy") could not speak, the narrator says (C¶19–21).
- **Henry Bellingham Somerville was born on 23 December 1848**, not 1859 as a "twin". The compiled genealogy's error is now refuted by the pedigree, the narrator ("elder brother"), and The Times's "fourth son".
- **Erina** married the **Rev. Thomas Forster Rolfe**, not "Woulfe", in 1883. He was Rector of Kirk Bramwith near Doncaster, which explains her death there.
- Further corrections from the pedigree:
  - Captain William's birth day: **7 March**, not Stirnet's 3 March or the tree's 17 March.
  - Bellingham (1810) died on 17 Nov **1844**, not 1845.
  - Dorothy died on **6** March 1895.
  - **Alan Everard** was buried in **Wicklow** churchyard, not at Queenstown.
  - William Henry's wife was **Henrietta Ryves**.
  - Eliza O'Connor was born in **1819**; the tree has 1815.
  - Corry died "in America" on **20 Jan 1879**; the compiled genealogy has Cleveland, 12 Jan 1880. This is kept as a conflict.
- **Elizabeth Tenison's father** is "Richard Tenison of Thomastown, co. Louth", very probably the son of Judge Thomas Tennison. This replaces the tree's impossible Richard (1685–1725).

## 5. Resolved questions

- Reginald's birth: **14 February 1897**, at Clermont.
- Who was the eighth son: Tenison, born and died in August 1812.
- Dorothea was the eldest sister.
- The children of Tenison Alan.
- Captain William's birth.
- The "Quentin Somerville debacle".
- Which Bellingham had the freedom of Dublin: four of them did, in 1842, 1883 and 1893.

Partly resolved:
- Tenison Alan's death (the date is secure; whether his wife died after him is still open).
- The papers in the narrator's keeping.
- The Clinch inheritance (Margaret was the second daughter, so there was an elder sister).
