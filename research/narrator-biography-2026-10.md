# The narrator's biography and the 1983 kidnapping (October 2026)

The story **The Narrator: a life of William Somerville** (`data/narrative.json`, id `the-narrator`; on the site at `#/story/the-narrator`) is a supplementary biography of Grandpa. It is marked as a later synthesis, not his own account. The transcripts and the existing chapters are unchanged.

It was commissioned with a focus on "his reported kidnapping in County Wicklow in 1984". The research found that the kidnapping took place on **10 August 1983**. 1984 was the year the kidnappers were convicted.

## What was found

| Source | Kind | Access | What it gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| *The People (DPP) v Prunty* [1986] ILRM 716, Court of Criminal Appeal, 15 May 1986 (`dpp-v-prunty-1986`) | Reported judgment | Read on vLex; the text there is cut short | The date, 10 August 1983: "taken by force from his home at Enniskerry"; held "approximately twenty four hours"; a taped ransom call at 5 p.m. on 11 August; a ransom "deposited at a quarry near to his home"; Michael Boyle pleaded guilty; a second man convicted, then the conviction set aside and a new trial ordered |
| John Maher, *The Irish Times*, 29 March 1997 (`it-maher-1997`) | Newspaper profile of Boyle | Read directly | The kidnappers meant to take the 14-year-old son, but the father persuaded them to take him instead. £50,000 demanded. Gardaí taped the calls and recognised the voices. He was found taped to a tree with a plastic bag over his head. Convictions in July 1984; Boyle got 12 years |
| Peter Murtagh, *The Irish Times*, 27 June 2008 (`it-murtagh-2008`) | Retrospective | Read directly | Places the case "a few days" after the failed Galen Weston kidnap attempt (August 1983). Held tied to a tree at the Army firing range, Kilpedder, for 24 hours |
| *Bray People*, August 1983, reprinted 2017 and 2018 (`bray-people-1983`) | Local weekly | Blocked to automated reading (HTTP 403); known only from search-engine extracts | James (14) found tied up by "a lone gunman … dressed in paramilitary uniform, masked and wielding a sawn-off shotgun", who demanded £50,000. According to Mr Somerville and the gardaí, no ransom was paid |
| RTÉ Archives, "Kidnappings in Ireland" (`rte-kidnappings`) | TV report, catalogue entry only | Read directly | A 1983 round-up of kidnappings. The description does not mention the case; the footage was not viewed |

**Disagreements, kept in the chapter:**
- **Length of captivity:** about 24 hours (court, *Bray People*, 2008) against "two days" (1997).
- **Where he was held:** the Army firing range at Kilpedder against "a forest five miles from the Somerville home".
- **Whether a ransom was paid:** "deposited at a quarry" (court) and the family "organised payment" (1997) against "no ransom was paid" (*Bray People*, citing him and the gardaí).

**The second accused** is not named in the chapter. His conviction was set aside in 1986 and no result of a retrial was found. His name appears only in the law report's title, in the source list.

**Grandpa does not mention the kidnapping** in any of the three recordings, or in his profile.

## The wider life

The career outline comes from his own entry in the compiled profiles (0682). Its source line is "WBRBS", his initials. It has not been confirmed independently:

- **Education:** Heronwater; St Columba's; St Andrew's; Trinity College Dublin (Classics, 1959).
- **Tanganyika:** HM Overseas Civil Service from 1959. District Officer and District Commissioner at Kondoa; magistrate in 1962. Independence fell on 9 December 1961.
- **Law:** at A&L Goodbody from 1964 to 2005, as partner, then chairman (1983–1990), then consultant.
- **Family:** married Manon Brittain in 1968; she founded the Rossana Arabian Stud.

Searches for independent confirmation of these points found nothing.

**A conflict to note:** James's birth is given as 1969 in his own profile and 1968 in his mother's. The 1983 report that he was 14 supports 1969.

## Images

**Not obtained:** no original newspaper page, scan or photograph of the kidnapping.

- **Where they would be:** the Irish newspapers of 11–20 August 1983, July 1984 and May 1986 are on subscription archives:
  - Irish Newspaper Archives (*Irish Press*, *Irish Independent*, *Evening Herald*);
  - *The Irish Times* archive;
  - PressReader (*Bray People*).
- **Why I couldn't reach them:** these services refused automated access, and the work did not try to get round that.
- **Rights:** any scans would be copyrighted. They could be described and cited, but not republished in this public repository without permission.

The chapter therefore uses only openly licensed photographs, each recorded in `data/images.json`. Every entry records the creator, date, licence and source, and states that it is not a family photograph:

## Unresolved questions

1. **The 1983 reporting.** What did the national dailies print on 11–13 August 1983, and with what photographs? Reading the original *Bray People* page would settle the quotation and any picture.
2. **The retrial.** What was the outcome of the retrial ordered in May 1986?
3. **The ransom.** Was one actually handed over? The sources conflict.
4. **Where he was held.** Was it the Army range at Kilpedder or "a forest five miles from the Somerville home"? These may be the same place.
5. **The RTÉ report.** Does Mary Fanning's 1983 report mention the case?
6. **The family's own memory.** Grandpa's own account of the night has not been recorded. Neither has the memory of James, Manon or the gardaí involved.
7. **His career.** No independent confirmation of it was found: A&L Goodbody's records, the Colonial Office lists, or the Trinity College registers would settle it.
